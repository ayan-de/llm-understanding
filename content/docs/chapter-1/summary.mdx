## Key ideas

- **LLMs transformed NLP.** Earlier approaches relied on hand-written rules and
  statistical tricks. LLMs replaced them with deep-learning models that understand,
  generate, and translate human language at a level nobody had seen before.
- **Two-stage training.** Modern LLMs are first *pretrained* on a huge unlabelled
  corpus using next-word prediction, then *finetuned* on smaller labelled data to
  follow instructions or classify text.
- **Transformers power them.** The transformer's *attention mechanism* gives the LLM
  selective access to the whole input sequence while generating output one word at a
  time.
- **Encoder vs decoder.** The original transformer has both. Text-generation LLMs like
  GPT-3 and ChatGPT keep only the decoder, which keeps the architecture simple and
  scaled.
- **Scale matters.** Pretraining needs billions of words. Here we'll implement training
  on small datasets and also learn how to load openly available model weights.
- **Emergent behaviour.** Despite being trained for "just" next-word prediction, GPT
  models can classify, translate, summarise, and more.
- **Finetuning wins.** A finetuned foundation model often beats a general-purpose LLM
  on a narrow task.
- **Build your own.** Custom LLMs unlock privacy, lower latency, and full control.

## Mental model to take forward

```
Pretraining on giant text  →  Foundation model
                                   │
                                   ├─ Instruction finetune  →  Chatbot / assistant
                                   └─ Classification finetune →  Spam / sentiment / etc.
```

That's the entire shape of modern LLM engineering, and the sections ahead walk through
every box on the diagram in PyTorch.
