## Why build your own LLM?

Writing an LLM from scratch is the best way to understand how it actually works — and
what it can't do. Once you do, you can also **pretrain or finetune** an existing
open-source model on your own data.

Custom-built LLMs often beat general-purpose ones on a specific task. Common examples
across industries:

- **Financial-domain LLMs** — tuned on filings, reports, and market text to assist with
  analysis and summarisation in finance.
- **Clinical / biomedical LLMs** — tuned on medical literature and Q&A datasets to help
  answer clinical questions.

There are three big reasons a company would want its own LLM:

1. **Privacy.** Sensitive data never has to leave the building.
2. **Latency & cost.** A small model can run on a laptop or phone, skipping the cloud.
3. **Control.** You decide exactly when the model gets updated and how it behaves.

## The two big stages: pretraining, then finetuning

Almost every modern LLM is built in two stages:

### 1. Pretraining — learn language broadly

- Train on a *huge* corpus of raw text (no labels needed).
- The task: **predict the next word**.
- Output: a "**base**" or "**foundation**" model — for example the original GPT-3.
- The base model can finish sentences and do simple few-shot tasks.

### 2. Finetuning — specialise on your task

- Take the pretrained base model.
- Train it further on a smaller, *labelled* dataset that's specific to what you want.
- Two popular flavours:
  - **Instruction finetuning** — pairs of instructions and answers (translate *this*
    text → expected translation). This is the recipe used to turn a base model into an
    instruction-following assistant.
  - **Classification finetuning** — text + class labels (spam vs not-spam, positive vs
    negative review, etc).

> **Quick aside** — the next-word pretraining task is a form of *self-supervised*
> learning. We don't manually label anything; the structure of the text itself provides
> the label (the next word). That's how we can throw enormous, unlabelled datasets at
> the model.
