---
title: 1.4 — Introducing the transformer architecture
description: Encoders, decoders, and the attention mechanism that powers every modern LLM.
---

Most modern LLMs are built on the **transformer** architecture, introduced in the
2017 paper *Attention Is All You Need*. To understand LLMs we have to take a quick
look at the original transformer — it was first designed for translating English text
into German and French.

## The original transformer in one sentence

The transformer is a **deep neural network with two halves**: an **encoder** that
reads and digests the input, and a **decoder** that produces the output one word at a
time.

For a translation task:

1. The encoder turns the source sentence ("This is an example") into a sequence of
   numerical vectors — a kind of *fingerprint* that captures meaning and context.
2. The decoder reads those vectors and writes the translated sentence word by word.

Both halves are connected by a mechanism called **self-attention**, which lets the
model decide which words in the input matter most for any given decision.

> Self-attention is the star of the show, and we're going to implement it ourselves in
> Chapter 3. For now, just remember: it lets a model weigh the importance of every
> word in a sequence against every other word.

## Two famous descendants: BERT and GPT

The transformer is a toolbox, and researchers built different tools from different
parts of it:

- **BERT** — built from the *encoder* part. Trained by hiding some words in a sentence
  and asking the model to fill them in. Great for understanding tasks like text
  classification; BERT-style models are widely used in production for content
  moderation, sentiment analysis, and search relevance.
- **GPT** — built from the *decoder* part. Trained to predict the next word. Great for
  generating text — translation, summarisation, fiction, code, you name it.

These notes focus on **GPT**, because that's how you build something that *generates*
text.

## Bonus superpowers of GPT

GPT models are trained just for next-word prediction, but they end up surprisingly
versatile:

- **Zero-shot learning** — solve a brand-new task with no examples at all.
- **Few-shot learning** — solve a new task after seeing only a handful of examples in
  the prompt.

> **Transformers vs LLMs** — the two terms get used interchangeably a lot, but they
> aren't identical. Transformers are a *family* of architectures (also used in vision).
> LLMs are language models built on top of them. And there *are* large language models
> that aren't transformers (built from RNNs or CNNs), though they are less common. In
> these notes, "LLM" means transformer-based LLM, GPT-style.
